# TV Music Store — Growth Funnel & Acquisition (draft)

> A pretty site with no funnel sells nothing. This is the plan for how strangers
> become paying, repeat customers. Draft to iterate on with the owner.

## The funnel at a glance

Awareness → Free signup → Activation (first download) → Paid → Retain/Expand → Refer

The **Free plan is the engine**: it's the lead magnet. Free users cost little,
give us their email, and become the pool we convert and market to.

## 1. Awareness (top) — how people find us

- **The music itself on YouTube.** Post tracks (or previews) on a TV Music Store
  YouTube channel, each description linking to its track page. Creators search
  YouTube for music constantly — this is the #1 channel for this niche.
- **Track-page SEO.** Every track page targets "<genre/mood> royalty-free music",
  "music for <use case>" (trailers, vlogs, ads). Clean titles, tags, descriptions,
  fast pages. This is a long-term compounding channel.
- **Free tools as traffic magnets** (the tunetank playbook): small free utilities
  creators search for (YouTube tag/description generators, thumbnail helpers,
  a "Content ID claim" explainer). They rank, pull traffic, and funnel to signup.
- **Composer audiences.** Our 3 composers cross-post/collab; their followers are
  pre-qualified.
- **Social + shorts.** Short clips of tracks over stock video, with a link.

## 2. Signup (capture) — turn a visitor into an email

- Gate downloads behind a free account (already the model). Downloading = signup.
- Offer the Free plan clearly on pricing and on every track ("Download free").
- Capture email early; set expectations (occasional new-release emails, unsubscribe
  anytime — matches the Privacy Policy).

## 3. Activation — first successful download

- Make the first download effortless (few clicks, clear license, PDF certificate).
- The certificate + "resolve Content ID claims" message builds trust immediately —
  this is a big differentiator vs. random free music.
- Onboarding email: how licensing works, attribution on Free, and what upgrading
  unlocks.

## 4. Conversion (Free → Paid) — the money step

Convert with **friction that upgrading removes**, not nags:
- Free download limits (e.g. 3/month) → "Upgrade for unlimited".
- WAV + stems, no attribution required, and **channel whitelisting** are paid-only.
- Contextual prompts at the moment of need (hitting the limit, wanting WAV, a claim
  on a video).
- One-time **sync licenses** for people who need one track for one project but
  won't subscribe — capture that intent too.
- Clear, simple pricing (we already have `/pricing` + `/licensing`).

## 5. Retention & expansion — where profit actually is

- **New-release emails by taste** (the admin CRM idea): segment by the genres/moods
  a customer downloads and email them relevant new tracks/albums (e.g. epic fans →
  new epic album). Far higher conversion than blast emails.
- Plan upgrades (Pro → Max) when their needs grow (client work, more channels).
- Win-back emails for cancelled subs.
- Keep churn low: the "keep your licenses forever" (perpetual) promise reduces
  cancel anxiety.

## 6. Referral / loyalty (later)

- Creator/affiliate program (commission for referrals).
- "Credit us" links from Free users are themselves free advertising.

## What to measure

- Visitors → signups (signup rate)
- Signups → first download (activation)
- Free → paid (conversion %) and time-to-convert
- MRR, churn %, and LTV (lifetime value) per customer
- Which acquisition channel drives the best LTV (double down there)
