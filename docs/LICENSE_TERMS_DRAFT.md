## 3. Permitted vs. not permitted

**Permitted (within your tier/plan scope):**

- Synchronizing the track with your video, film, podcast, game, app, or audio
  project, as allowed by your license type.
- Monetized content on platforms you're allowed to use (e.g. YouTube, Vimeo,
  social media, streaming).
- Editing, trimming, looping, and mixing the track to fit your project (but not
  isolating stems for resale or re-distribution as music).

**Not permitted (any tier):**

- Reselling, redistributing, sharing, or making the track available as a
  standalone audio file, sample, or in a music library.
- Registering the track (or your video's audio) with any Content ID or rights
  management system as **your** content.
- Claiming authorship or ownership of the music.
- Using the track in a defamatory, hateful, pornographic, or illegal context.
- Trademark, logo, or "sonic brand" registration of the track.
- Exclusive use or buy-outs (not offered).
- Any use beyond the scope of your specific tier/plan (e.g. broadcast on a
  Commercial license, or multiple projects on a single one-time license — each
  project needs its own license).

## 4. Perpetuity & cancellation

- **One-time licenses** are perpetual for the single project they were bought
  for. They never expire.
- **Subscription licenses**: any track you licensed/downloaded while your
  subscription was active stays licensed **forever** for the projects you used
  it in — cancelling only stops you from licensing **new** tracks. Your existing
  videos/projects never become infringing because you cancelled.
- If a payment is reversed, charged back, or fails (see §7), the affected license
  is suspended until the payment is resolved.

The License Certificate does **not** show an expiry date, because licenses do not
expire. It records the issue date and, for subscriptions, the plan under which it
was granted.

## 6. YouTube Content ID & claims

Our catalog is protected through YouTube's Content ID system (administered on our
behalf by third-party rights-management services). When a track is used, YouTube
may automatically place a **Content ID claim** on the video. A claim is normally
just a notice/monetization flag — it is not a copyright strike.

We release claims for licensed uses in two ways:

- **Subscription plans — channel whitelisting.** Add your YouTube channel(s), up
  to your plan's limit, in your account. While your subscription is **active** we
  clear Content ID claims on videos on those channels that use our music. Anything
  published while active stays cleared; videos published **after** the subscription
  ends are not covered. (Operationally this is managed by us per the whitelist —
  see `WHITELIST_SYSTEM.md`.)
- **One-time licenses / single claims.** Your **License Certificate** is your
  proof — send us the **License Number** and the video link at
  contact@tvmusicstore.com and we release that specific claim.

Releasing a claim does **not** transfer any ownership — the music always remains
the intellectual property of TV Music Store and its composers.

- **Refunds:** All sales are **final** (digital goods delivered immediately),

  wrong file) within **48 hours** and we cannot fix it within **5 business days**,
